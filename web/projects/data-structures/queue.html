Data Structures

Queues

A queue is an abstract data structure based on an array, that operates on a first-in, first-out basis (FIFO). Items are added (enqueued) at the back of the queue, and removed (dequeued) from the front.

Queues are used to schedule tasks to be executed by the CPU, handle requests to servers, and traverse graphs breadth-first.

In a real-life queue, people move forwards when the person at the front is served/dequeued. While this is possible to implement on a computer, it is very inefficient and scales poorly as the queue gets longer. Instead of moving the items forwards, it is much easier and faster to move the front of the queue backwards, and loop around if it runs out of space. For this reason, queues have two pointers, one to the back of the queue for adding items, and one to the front for removing them.
